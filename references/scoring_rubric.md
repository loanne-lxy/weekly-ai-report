# Evidence-based value scoring

Score importance, novelty and impact independently in [0,1], using the supplied articles only.
These are VALUE dimensions, not publication recency, publisher reputation, popularity or confidence.
A solid benchmark, engineering system, replication with new findings or incremental method can
have substantial value without a new architecture. Judge each study within its stated problem;
a narrow scientific field is not automatically low impact. Do not force a score distribution.

| Dimension | 0.2 | 0.4 | 0.6 | 0.8 | 0.9-1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| importance | minor change, little demonstrated utility | useful but limited contribution | solid demonstrated solution to a meaningful problem | substantial advance with strong comparative or operational evidence | exceptional, broadly consequential contribution with unusually strong support |
| novelty | routine reuse or replication without new findings | modest adaptation or limited evidence of differentiation | clear new method, evaluation, integration or finding relative to an identified baseline | substantial demonstrated methodological or capability difference | exceptional new direction, supported by explicit prior-work comparison |
| impact | little evidenced applicability | bounded pilot or plausible but untested utility | meaningful validated benefit in its target setting, or a reusable tool/evaluation with demonstrated use | substantial benefit across tasks, settings or real deployments | exceptional demonstrated reach and magnitude |

Use intermediate values when justified. A performance percentage alone does not determine a band:
consider baseline strength, absolute benefit, scope and validation. A product announcement or a
large parameter count alone does not justify a high score. Different papers in a research theme
must not be treated as replications of each other; article count does not establish stronger evidence.
For unknown novelty, state that comparative evidence is missing and avoid confident novelty claims.
Separate prospective impact from demonstrated impact; do not infer industry adoption from a paper.
Each Chinese rationale must name a supporting article/method and explain the chosen band and scope.
Never invent baselines, validation, first-ever claims or causal effects. No score uplift merely for a priority topic.
