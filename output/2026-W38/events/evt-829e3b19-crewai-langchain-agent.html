Its Simcenter Executable Digital Twin, or xDT, combines physics-based simulation, artificial intelligence, and real-time operational data to help industrial teams detect problems, test scenarios, and optimize assets while they are running. Industrial facilities typically maintain separate streams of operational technology, business information, and external data such as weather or grid conditions. Siemens aims to connect these sources so engineers can uncover relationships that conventional monitoring systems may miss. The xDT runs alongside physical equipment and calculates conditions that sensors cannot directly measure. The approach addresses limitations in both traditional simulation and AI. High-fidelity multiphysics simulations can provide accurate engineering predictions but may require significant computing time. AI models respond quickly but can produce results that violate physical constraints. Siemens combines reduced-order modeling with AI to compress detailed Simcenter simulations into lightweight executable models that can operate at real-time speeds. The architecture has three main elements: a Unified Namespace that organizes operational information, the executable digital twin that provides physics-based predictions, and AI agents with knowledge graphs that analyze relationships and identify anomalies.
