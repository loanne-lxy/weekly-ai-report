A1Make It Flow: Animating Water in 3D Gaussian Splatting Scenes

Explicit 3D scene representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have recently enabled high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis. However, these methods assume static scenes, limiting realism in the presence of dynamic phenomena ubiquitous in real-world environments. While coherent motion (e.g., humans or vehicles) can be captured and reconstructed, high-frequency effects such as flowing water remain intractable under casual capture conditions, where dense multi-view, high-speed acquisition is not feasible. We present a method to animate water in static 3DGS scenes, enabling visually plausible motion without requiring dynamic capture or physical simulation. Our approach estimates 2D optical flow from reference images or model renderings and lifts it into a consistent 3D Eulerian motion field. We propose a cinemagraph-inspired algorithm that applies this motion field to deform Gaussian primitives, producing temporally seamless looping animations. The resulting representation retains real-time rendering performance, significantly enhances perceptual realism, and preserves free-viewpoint navigation within the scene. We evaluate our method on a newly captured dataset of in-the-wild waterfall scenes and demonstrate clear improvements over prior work in dynamic Gaussian splatting and 3D cinemagraph generation in visual quality and computational efficiency.

A23DRAG: 3D-Native Object Drag Editing with Generative Models

The rapid progress of 3D generative models has made 3D asset creation increasingly accessible. However, achieving intuitive, precise, and interactive user control, such as point-based drag editing, remains challenging. Existing 3D drag methods either rely on slow 2D diffusion priors, such as Score Distillation Sampling, or directly warp geometry, often causing multi-view inconsistency and topological artifacts. In this paper, we present 3DRAG, a training-free framework that executes precise drag editing natively within the explicit 3D latent space of large-scale generative models, without SDS-based optimization or iterative point tracking. To ensure geometrically plausible deformations, 3DRAG explicitly extends the drag displacement field into 3D space, incorporating an Occupancy-Aware Winner-Takes-All (OA-WTA) strategy to robustly handle sparse geometries. Furthermore, we introduce a generative editing pipeline that explicitly transforms latent representations via the 3D displacement field. By fully leveraging the model's generative capabilities, it achieves smooth geometric deformations while maintaining the object's identity. To further transfer original geometry and texture with high fidelity, we propose Logits-Level Token Fusion, which accurately injects source information into the destination region. To evaluate 3D point-based editing, we construct a novel dataset featuring diverse 3D assets and complex multi-point drag instructions. Quantitative and qualitative experiments demonstrate that 3DRAG achieves state-of-the-art performance, surpassing existing baselines in drag accuracy, geometric fidelity, and unedited region preservation.

A3OuroWorld: Bringing Any 3D World Alive as Diverse, Endlessly Looping 3D Cinemagraphs

Recent 3D world models generate photorealistic, explorable scenes that remain frozen in time. OuroWorld is a mask-free framework that turns any static 3D Gaussian Splatting scene into a 3D cinemagraph: a dynamic scene with vivid, diverse motion looping seamlessly from any viewpoint. A vision-language model infers plausible dynamics and guides a video model to synthesize a reference video, which we lift and complete into multi-view videos. To learn from this imperfect supervision, we propose Inconsistency-Robust Periodic 4DGS: a Fourier-series deformation field guarantees looping by construction, while a Grounded Drift Field anchored at the reference view absorbs cross-view inconsistency. Unlike prior Eulerian methods limited to fluid-like motion, we capture general deformation, object motion, and illumination change. We introduce a ground-truth-free evaluation covering vividness, naturalness, loop seam coherence, and scene quality. On 39 reconstructed and generated scenes, OuroWorld outperforms all baselines and wins 70.8%-99.0% of user-study comparisons. Project page: https://ouroworld.userwei.com

A4HorizonNav: Geometry-Guided Video Diffusion for Anticipatory 3D Robot Navigation

We present HorizonNav, a video diffusion-based framework for anticipatory robot navigation that transforms a single RGB image and a text prompt into a geometrically aligned representation of plausible surrounding 3D space. Given the initial observation and a textual description of the reconstruction objective, a vision-language model, guided by designed instructions, interprets the scene and constructs structured prompts for a video diffusion model. The resulting video sequence provides plausible future observations of the environment, which are subsequently processed using monocular ORB-SLAM3 for camera pose estimation and Video Depth Anything for dense depth prediction. Our central contribution is a geometry-guided depth alignment stage that combines the estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to constrain dense depth predictions across multiple views. This alignment improves the geometric consistency of the reconstructed 3D environment, addressing inconsistencies that temporal coherence of the generated video alone cannot resolve. The experimental evaluation of navigation in imagined 3D environment shows that across a 19-scene horizon study, planning-feasible scenes increase from 13/19 at P20 to 15/19 at P81, while successful RRT trials increase from 62/90 to 75/90. In a separate corridor study, 6 of 25 evaluable generated-camera trajectories violate the reconstructed collision constraints; RRT planner recovers feasible alternatives in 5 of those 6 cases. A larger archive contains 58 usable reconstructions from 67 attempted cases, of which 36 support a downstream plan and 22 do not, exposing reconstruction and free-space failure modes rather than hiding them without lidar. Across 15 corridor reconstructions, the mean reconstruction error is approximately 0.30 m at matched structural landmarks.
